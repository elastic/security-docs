=== GitHub OAuth App Access Restrictions Disabled

Detects when OAuth App access restrictions are disabled for a GitHub organization. With restrictions enabled, only owner-approved OAuth Apps can access organization data; once disabled, any OAuth App that a member authorizes gains access without owner review. Following an account takeover, an adversary may disable restrictions and then authorize a malicious or attacker-controlled OAuth App to establish token-based persistence and exfiltrate repository data, making this a high-impact change to the organization's third-party access controls.

*Investigating GitHub OAuth App Access Restrictions Disabled*


This rule triggers on `org.disable_oauth_app_restrictions`, which turns off the organization control that limits third-party OAuth App access to owner-approved apps. Once disabled, any OAuth App a member authorizes can access organization data without owner review. An adversary who has taken over an owner account commonly chains this with `oauth_authorization.create` to grant a malicious OAuth App broad, token-based access to repositories and organization resources, establishing persistence and a data-exfiltration path that survives password and session resets.


*Possible investigation steps*


- Identify the actor from `user.name`, `github.actor_ip`, `github.user_agent`, and `github.programmatic_access_type`, and confirm whether they legitimately hold organization owner privileges.
- Determine whether the change was expected by checking change-management records and the affected `github.org`, and confirm whether OAuth App restrictions were previously enforced.
- Review OAuth authorization events (`oauth_authorization.create`) and third-party app grants created after the disable timestamp, prioritizing apps with broad scopes or unfamiliar publishers.
- Review authentication context for the actor around the event time for signs of takeover, such as sign-ins from new `github.actor_ip` addresses, unfamiliar user agents, or token-based access (`github.programmatic_access_type`) instead of normal interactive activity.
- Correlate for other high-impact identity or policy changes near the same timestamp, including owner or member additions, PAT grants, SSO/SAML changes, and 2FA requirement changes.


*False positive analysis*


- An organization owner temporarily disables restrictions to onboard an approved vendor app; this should be ticketed and followed by re-enabling restrictions and explicitly approving the specific app instead.
- A planned governance or migration change performed by a known administrator can generate this event as expected activity.


*Response and remediation*


- Re-enable OAuth App access restrictions for the organization as soon as the change is confirmed unauthorized, and approve required apps individually rather than leaving restrictions off.
- Review and revoke OAuth App authorizations granted after the disable event, especially apps with broad scopes or no business justification.
- Rotate credentials and tokens potentially exposed through authorized apps, including PATs, OAuth grants, and any secrets accessible to the granted apps.
- Review org audit logs for other changes made by the actor after the disable event, and revert any unauthorized modifications.
- Escalate to incident response if the actor is unknown or unauthorized, or if the disable event is followed by new OAuth grants, owner additions, or sign-ins from unfamiliar infrastructure.
- Harden by enforcing OAuth App access restrictions org-wide, restricting who can change organization security settings, and alerting on future changes to OAuth App restrictions and new third-party app authorizations.
